The global economy recovered from the immediate COVID-19 shock, but it did not reset to the same operating conditions. Output returned faster than all the jobs, firms, incomes, and productive relationships disrupted by the crisis. Inflation moved from a demand collapse to a supply-constrained reopening. Policy moved from emergency support to restraint. Medium-term growth remained below the longer-run pre-pandemic average [S1][S2][S3][S4].

The durable response is a practical one:

- protect critical supply, labor, and financial dependencies;
- make scenario planning part of normal governance;
- measure recovery through distribution as well as aggregate output;
- preserve the capacity to invest through the next shock;
- treat resilience as an economic capability, not only a risk-control expense.

The central lesson is simple: a rebound tells us that activity can restart. It does not tell us that the economy has become less fragile. That distinction is the difference between reporting recovery and preparing for the next cycle.
