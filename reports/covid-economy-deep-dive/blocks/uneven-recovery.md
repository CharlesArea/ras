The headline rebound can create a false sense of completion. The World Bank's early recovery work emphasized that the pace of recovery differed across economies, while the ILO described unequal effects across workers and sectors [S2][S6]. A global average can therefore improve while a meaningful share of people and firms remain below their pre-pandemic trajectory.

## Why divergence widened

Recovery capacity depended on several pre-existing conditions:

1. **Fiscal space:** governments with room to support incomes and firms could reduce unnecessary scarring.
2. **Health and vaccination capacity:** reopening depended on the ability to reduce health risk and restore mobility.
3. **Sector mix:** economies dependent on tourism, hospitality, transport, or face-to-face services were exposed to a longer interruption.
4. **Digital and organizational flexibility:** some work and commerce could move online; other activity could not.
5. **Access to finance:** firms with weak cash buffers could fail before demand returned.

The differences matter for development. The World Bank's medium-term framing warns that a period of low global growth is not enough to restore lost development momentum [S4]. If investment, education, labor-force attachment, or firm formation remain below trend, the cost of the shock can persist after the initial output gap closes.

## The measurement rule

Any serious post-COVID assessment should report both the level and the distribution: how much aggregate output returned, who regained work, which firms survived, and which groups remain further from the counterfactual path. Without that split, policy can declare success while leaving the most exposed parts of the economy unmeasured.
