The global economy did not move directly from “before” to “after” COVID-19. It passed through a sequence of distinct regimes: a pre-pandemic slowdown, a synchronized shutdown shock, a policy-supported reopening rebound, and a more fragile adjustment period in which inflation, supply constraints, labor-market scarring, and geopolitical risk mattered more than they had in the old baseline [S1][S2][S3][S4].

## The report's answer in five findings

1. **The pandemic interrupted an economy that was already losing momentum.** World real GDP growth declined from 3.8% in 2017 to 2.8% in 2019. The shock therefore landed on top of an existing slowdown rather than on an economy accelerating at its long-run average [S1].
2. **2020 was larger than a conventional recession.** Output fell, but so did mobility, service activity, working hours, supply reliability, and the solvency of exposed firms. The ILO estimated an 8.8% loss of global working hours, equivalent to 255 million full-time jobs [S2].
3. **The 2021 rebound was real but partly mechanical.** Growth rose to 6.5% in the IMF April 2024 series, reflecting the reopening of economies and the comparison with the exceptionally low 2020 base [S1].
4. **Inflation became a central constraint.** The economy moved from early-pandemic demand weakness and falling oil prices to reopening demand, bottlenecks, energy pressure, and food-price pressure [S5].
5. **The post-COVID baseline is lower-growth and higher-friction.** Growth around 3.2–3.5% in 2022–2024 remained below the 2000–2019 average of about 3.8% in the selected IMF vintage, while the World Bank continued to flag policy, trade, geopolitical, inflation, and climate headwinds [S1][S4].

## Central implication

The durable economic change is not simply the size of the 2020 contraction. It is the loss of certainty about how quickly supply, labor, prices, and policy can normalize after a shock. Planning now requires more scenario work, more resilience in critical dependencies, and a wider definition of recovery than GDP alone.
