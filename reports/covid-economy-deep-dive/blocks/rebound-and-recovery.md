The global economy rebounded strongly in 2021. In the IMF April 2024 statistical series, world real GDP growth reached 6.5% after the 2020 contraction [S1]. The World Bank's early recovery assessment also described a powerful rebound, while warning that the speed and completeness of recovery varied by country and sector [S6].

The rebound had three components. First, restrictions eased and activity returned in sectors that had been closed. Second, households and firms released some demand that had been postponed. Third, the percentage change was calculated against a depressed 2020 base. These mechanisms can produce a large one-year growth number without establishing a permanently higher trend.

## Reopening is not the same as repair

Reopening repaired the flow of activity, but not automatically the stock of damage. A worker who lost a job, a small firm that exhausted its cash buffer, or a household that accumulated debt could remain weaker after the economy reopened. The ILO warned about longer-term scarring and inequality risks, especially where labor-market support and access to opportunity were limited [S2].

The policy challenge was therefore dual: support the immediate restart while protecting the productive and human capacity needed for the next cycle. Too little support risked unnecessary firm closures and labor-market detachment. Too much broad stimulus after supply had become constrained risked adding to inflation pressure. That tension shaped the next phase.
