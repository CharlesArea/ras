The global economy moved through four distinct phases: a pre-COVID slowdown, a sudden 2020 contraction, a powerful but uneven 2021 rebound, and a post-COVID adjustment with lower growth and a more difficult inflation and policy environment [S1][S3].

## The short answer

- **Before COVID:** Growth was positive but already cooling, from 3.8% in 2017 to 2.8% in 2019 [S1].
- **During the shock:** Global real GDP fell 2.7% in 2020, while the ILO estimated an 8.8% loss of global working hours, equivalent to 255 million full-time jobs [S1][S2].
- **During the rebound:** Growth jumped to 6.5% in 2021, but the recovery was uneven across workers, firms, and countries [S1][S2].
- **After the rebound:** Growth normalized to roughly 3.2–3.5% in 2022–2024 in the IMF April 2024 vintage, below the 2000–2019 average of about 3.8% [S1].

## Bottom line

COVID-19 did not simply create one bad year. It reset the operating environment: supply resilience became more important, inflation became a central policy constraint, work was reorganized unevenly, and the medium-term growth outlook became more fragile [S2][S3][S4].
