The post-COVID economy is best described as a lower-growth, higher-friction baseline rather than a return to the old normal. In the IMF April 2024 vintage, growth after the rebound was around 3.2–3.5%, below the 2000–2019 average of about 3.8% [S1].

The World Bank’s January 2025 outlook also highlighted persistent headwinds: policy uncertainty, trade-policy shifts, geopolitical tensions, inflation risks, and climate-related disruptions. Its message is that a stable but weak expansion is not enough to restore lost development momentum [S4].

## Practical interpretation

For organizations planning ahead, the evidence supports four priorities:

- **Build supply flexibility:** treat critical inputs and logistics as strategic dependencies.
- **Plan for rate and price volatility:** budgets should include scenarios for inflation and financing costs.
- **Measure recovery beyond output:** track employment quality, small-business survival, and distributional effects.
- **Avoid assuming a full reset:** the rebound was strong, but the World Bank’s early recovery assessment emphasized that it was uneven and exposed scarring risks [S6].

The central change is not that the world economy stopped growing. It is that growth now operates under a more fragile combination of supply, policy, labor, and geopolitical constraints.
