The labor-market shock was larger than the headline GDP number suggests. The ILO estimated that 8.8% of global working hours were lost in 2020, equivalent to 255 million full-time jobs. Global employment fell by 114 million compared with 2019, and the employment shortfall was 144 million jobs larger than a no-pandemic scenario [S2].

The impact was uneven. Workers with less protection, people in contact-intensive services, and smaller firms were generally more exposed to closures and reduced demand. The 2021 rebound therefore repaired aggregate output faster than it repaired every household’s income, job prospects, or business balance sheet [S2].

## A durable lesson

Economic resilience is not only the speed of GDP recovery. It also depends on whether workers can return to quality employment, whether viable firms survive the shock, and whether the recovery spreads beyond the sectors that reopen first.
