The price story changed direction twice. At the start of the pandemic, demand and oil prices fell sharply, pushing inflation down. As economies reopened, demand recovered faster than supply in many markets; supply bottlenecks, energy prices, and food pressures then pushed inflation sharply higher [S5].

The result was a harder policy trade-off than before COVID. The IMF’s April 2024 outlook placed global headline inflation at 6.8% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024, and 4.5% in 2025—declining, but still requiring restrictive policy in many economies and remaining above the pre-pandemic average for a time [S3].

## Why this matters

Businesses and households had to manage both sides of the shock: first an abrupt loss of demand and income, then higher input costs and borrowing costs during the recovery.
